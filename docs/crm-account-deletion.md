# CRM business account deletion

Status: staging-verified database corrections. The three applicable migrations were manually applied to CRM Production and their history reconciled on 2026-09-24. Full account deletion is not yet verified; this is not a self-service flow or Production runbook approval.

Release dependency: the Google grant-revocation fix is in draft PR #29 (`codex/expenses-production-release`, commit `200ccd6`), not in this PR. Do not release account deletion without that fix; `main` does not yet contain the Calendar integration.

## Scope

- Identify the exact CRM `auth.users.id` after verifying the requester's identity. Do not identify the account by an email appearing in a lead, payment event, or Trainer record.
- Supabase Auth user deletion does not itself invalidate already-issued JWTs immediately. Check the application's session checks and configured token lifetime; do not describe account deletion as instant token revocation.
- Cancel billing separately in Grow and record the paid-through date before closing access. Deleting a CRM account does not cancel a Grow mandate.
- Pause CRM-to-Activation dispatch and resolve in-flight deliveries before deleting the account. Local `client_activations` and child events belong to the CRM business ID and are deleted; Trainer clients are a separate service and must not be deleted automatically.
- If the account has a Google Calendar connection, revoke its Google grant before deleting the encrypted refresh token. Deleting the database connection alone is not evidence of remote revocation.
- Inventory any CRM Storage objects before Auth deletion. Use the Storage API to remove them; deleting `storage.objects` rows directly can orphan files. CRM Staging had no buckets on 2026-09-23.
- Preserve only the approved minimized Grow payment evidence, with no account `user_id` or raw provider payload. Payment-provider records and provider-managed or manual backups have independent retention rules.

## Backup retention policy

Approved by the operator on 2026-09-24. This approval sets retention periods; it does not authorize deletion of any existing file, change the existing Grow backup task, or approve publication of privacy wording. The policy is not yet enforced by an automated cleanup mechanism.

- Retain an encrypted manual CRM Production database backup for no more than seven days from the file's creation time. Use the timestamp recorded by the filesystem for the original backup, not the timestamp embedded in its filename. Copying a backup must not restart its retention period: the known OneDrive copy expires with its identical source backup.
- Retain a CRM Staging backup containing only synthetic QA data for no more than 48 hours from creation. Do not treat a backup containing real customer data as eligible for this synthetic-data rule; stop and assess it separately.
- An operational exception may extend retention to at most 30 days from original creation only with a documented reason, owner, approval, and fixed expiry. A legal preservation requirement must be assessed separately rather than assumed to fit the operational exception.
- Ron is responsible for a weekly inventory and isolated restore-readiness check, including every known local and synchronized copy. Record the check, its result, and any unresolved access or verification gap. Do not expose decrypted backup contents or credentials in the record.
- Record each authorized deletion with the exact file path or copy identifier, deletion time, reason, and approval. Before deleting an existing file, present the exact targets, ages, and impact for separate explicit approval. A file reaching its retention deadline is not, by itself, approval to delete it.
- Mark an inaccessible or unverified external copy as **unverified**, not deleted. Verify OneDrive cloud sync and version history independently before claiming that copy has expired or been removed.
- Handle the separate DPAPI-encrypted Grow backup under its pre-existing scheduled task, not under the manual full-database backup rule. The task was observed ready to run on 2026-09-29; this policy approval does not change or validate that task's execution.
- Account deletion affects active CRM records, not historical full-database snapshots. Do not promise immediate or total erasure from backups; inactive backup copies may persist until their approved expiry. They must not be used for routine operations. Any provider-held records or backups need their own verified retention terms.

Inventory at approval: five local encrypted full-database Production backups dated 2026-09-21 through 2026-09-23, one byte-identical copy of the first backup under a local OneDrive path, and one separate DPAPI-encrypted Grow backup. A separate manual upload of the first backup to OneDrive `hubianron@gmail.com` was downloaded and SHA-256-verified against the original on 2026-09-24; its visible version history showed only version 1.0. A verification download remains in `Downloads`. Sync of the different local OneDrive-folder copy to its configured account is still unverified. All known copies inherit the original's seven-day deadline; see the local `backup-inventory-20260924.md` for exact paths and times. The full-database backup script has no age-based cleanup, so the seven-day rule is a policy commitment that still needs an approved enforcement process.

## Database gate

The migration `20260923205606_crm_account_deletion_cascade.sql` makes local Activation records cascade with their CRM Auth business owner. It changes `business_center_content_items.user_id` to `ON DELETE CASCADE` when that table exists. It aborts if an Activation row has no matching Auth owner. The later `20260923223632_crm_content_deletion_fk.sql` requires both content tables, makes the content-owner FK cascade if still needed, and changes the attribution-to-content FK from `RESTRICT` to `NO ACTION DEFERRABLE INITIALLY DEFERRED`. This allows Auth deletion to cascade through both tables in one transaction while a standalone deletion of attributed content still fails at commit. Apply the original content-library and attribution schema migrations before the later migration on environments that lack those tables. The migration `20260924152712_roi_tools_account_deletion_cascade.sql` requires the ROI baseline table and adds a validated `roi_tools.user_id -> auth.users.id ON DELETE CASCADE` foreign key. It aborts rather than deleting or reassigning orphaned ROI rows.

Before the Production migrations, `business_center_content_items.user_id` used `ON DELETE NO ACTION`, `business_center_lead_attributions.content_item_id` used `ON DELETE RESTRICT`, and `roi_tools.user_id` had no foreign key. These three gaps were corrected on 2026-09-24 as recorded below. `client_activations` was absent in Production, so the Activation branch of the first migration did not run there. Do not run Production account deletion merely because these database constraints are now in place; the non-database gates below remain open.

Before any Production deletion, recheck every CRM-owned foreign key to `auth.users` and the indirect content/attribution relationship. Confirm the target business's Activation rows, Google connection state, Storage inventory, and minimized Grow payloads without exporting personal data. Obtain a recoverable backup and separate Production approval.

After the authorized Auth Admin deletion, verify zero target rows in `users`, `leads`, `tasks`, `roi_tools`, `business_center_content_items`, `business_center_lead_attributions`, `manual_expenses`, `google_calendar_connections`, `google_calendar_events`, `agent_integration_credentials`, `crm_client_activation_outbox`, `client_activations`, and `client_activation_events` (through the exact activation IDs). Check indirect lead-owned tables too. Confirm retained Grow rows have `user_id IS NULL` and only approved payment fields. Do not report backup, Google, Grow, or Trainer data as deleted merely because CRM Auth deletion succeeded.
